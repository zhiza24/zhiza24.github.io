# Capacity, role and collaboration map
**Status:** review-ready methodology and integration specification; not a claim that a new assessment or production feature is already implemented.
**Depends on:** `docs/GOAL_EXECUTION_PATTERN_MAP.md` (Goal execution patterns + Reactor Strategy Workshop).
**Purpose:** help the player choose not only an effective way to act, but also a realistic division of work between self, collaborators, specialists, services, and tools.

## 1. Core idea

People differ not only in their habits of moving toward a goal, but also in the functions they naturally notice, enjoy, perform reliably, or find costly to sustain.

One person may be strong at seeing the whole system, inventing a concept, connecting many dependencies, and designing what should exist. The same person may find routine administration, repeated follow-up, deadline control, and extracting status updates unusually draining or unreliable.

Another person may be strong at organizing people and commitments, clarifying who does what, following up, and bringing work to completion. That does not automatically mean they are the best person to invent the original concept or define its deeper direction.

A third person may be excellent at producing a concrete deliverable once the task and acceptance criteria are clear. They may not want to coordinate a team or carry strategic ambiguity.

The game should help a player see this as a question of **functional fit and resource allocation**, not as a hierarchy of human worth or a fixed personality type.

The key question is not only:
- "What should I do differently?"

It is also:
- "Which part is genuinely mine to do?"
- "Which part should be supported, delegated, automated, purchased as a service, learned, or removed from the plan?"
- "What kind of person or resource would complement this particular goal?"

## 2. Terminology: functions, not types

Use the terms **role**, **function**, **capacity**, **working preference**, and **support need**. Avoid declaring "you are a creator / administrator / executor" as an identity.

A person can perform several functions, change roles across projects, and develop skills. A role that feels natural in one setting may be difficult in another because of authority, incentives, workload, safety, available tools, or experience.

Never infer a person's capacity from one answer, psychotype, birth matrix, BG16, Echo code, or a single test. The player confirms or corrects the synthesis.

Separate five things that can look similar:
1. **Ability:** can the person perform the task to the required standard?
2. **Experience:** has the person done it before, with what result?
3. **Preference:** does the person want to do it?
4. **Capacity:** do they currently have time, energy, attention, health, money, and access?
5. **Role conditions:** do they have the authority, information, tools, and cooperation needed?

"I do not want to do this" is not the same as "I cannot do this." "I can do it" is not the same as "I should carry it indefinitely."

## 3. Candidate functional repertoire

This is a practical map of work functions, not a validated taxonomy and not a forced list of mutually exclusive categories. Use it as a vocabulary to describe tasks and identify missing support.

| Function | Typical contribution | Typical deliverables | Possible support need |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Creator / originator** | Sees possibilities, invents concepts, notices patterns, generates original directions | Concept, vision, product idea, creative brief, new approach | Operational partner, producer, administrator, implementation team |
| **Systems architect / strategist** | Sees dependencies, decomposes complexity, designs structure and sequence | Architecture, roadmap, principles, decision model, system map | Project coordination, specialist validation, execution capacity |
| **Producer / integrator** | Turns a broad direction into an organized project; aligns people, scope, timing, and dependencies | Work plan, owners, milestones, coordinated delivery | Clear authority, domain experts, executive support |
| **Administrator / operations coordinator** | Maintains order, schedules, records, follow-ups, recurring processes, and status visibility | Updated task board, meeting notes, reminders, reconciled records, status reports | Clear procedures, decision rights, realistic workload |
| **Executor / maker / specialist** | Performs a defined task with craft and consistency | Code, design, analysis, copy, build, service, completed task | Clear brief, acceptance criteria, feedback, tools |
| **Researcher / analyst** | Gathers information, compares evidence, checks assumptions, identifies uncertainty | Research brief, options, risks, evidence, recommendation | Decision owner, scope, deadline, access to sources |
| **Quality / verification function** | Tests whether work meets requirements and whether the intended result actually occurred | QA report, acceptance check, defect list, outcome evidence | Independent criteria, permission to report problems |
| **Communicator / negotiator** | Explains, asks, aligns expectations, negotiates, handles stakeholder contact | Agreement, request, proposal, clarified expectations | Authority boundaries, accurate information, support for difficult conversations |
| **Connector / relationship builder** | Finds people, opens doors, builds networks and partnerships | Introductions, partner pipeline, community, access | Clear proposition, follow-through owner |
| **Care / support function** | Maintains human continuity, onboarding, service, coordination of support | Customer or team support, care plan, onboarding, retention | Boundaries, adequate staffing, escalation path |
| **Decision owner / accountable lead** | Makes trade-offs, sets priorities, accepts responsibility for final choices | Decision log, priority, approved scope, escalation decision | Reliable evidence, defined remit, ability to say no |

### Important distinctions
- Creator and systems architect can overlap but are not identical: a person may invent an idea without designing its implementation, or design systems without originating the concept.
- Producer and administrator are not identical: the producer integrates delivery and resolves dependencies; the administrator maintains operational continuity and process details. A small project may combine them.
- Administrator and executor are not interchangeable: organizing the work is different from doing the specialist work.
- Quality/verification is not simply "being critical"; it is a defined responsibility to compare deliverables and outcomes with agreed criteria.
- A person can lead one area and be a contributor in another.
- "Getting results" must never mean coercion, humiliation, manipulation, unsafe pressure, or overriding consent. Accountability is based on clear agreements, fair follow-up, and transparent consequences.

## 4. How to map a player's functional fit

Do not add a new long personality test by default. First reuse evidence the player has already supplied and ask only for missing, goal-specific clarification.

For each relevant function, the internal synthesis may distinguish:
- demonstrated strength (with a concrete example);
- workable skill (can do it, but not necessarily preferred);
- energy cost / friction;
- current capacity and constraints;
- desire to develop the function;
- preference to delegate or obtain support;
- evidence confidence and date;
- player's confirmation, correction, or disagreement.

Use a small player-friendly scale only as a navigation aid, not as a psychometric score:
- "Это одна из моих сильных сторон"
- "Я умею, но это требует усилий"
- "Могу при подходящих условиях"
- "Предпочитаю передать эту часть"
- "Пока не знаю / зависит от проекта"

Ask for examples where a recommendation would materially affect the plan. Example prompts:
- "Какую часть этой работы ты обычно видишь раньше других?"
- "Что у тебя получается доводить до результата без постоянного внешнего контроля?"
- "Какая часть проекта чаще всего остается без внимания, хотя ты понимаешь, что она нужна?"
- "Что ты можешь делать, но после чего у тебя почти не остается сил на главное?"
- "Какие задачи ты уже успешно передавал другим?"
- "Если бы у тебя появился надежный помощник на несколько часов в неделю, какую задачу ты передал бы первой?"
- "Тебе сейчас важнее научиться выполнять эту функцию самому или найти способ не нести ее в одиночку?"

A player may answer "none", "depends", or "not enough experience". Do not force a profile or a dominant role.

## 5. Goal-specific application

Functional fit must be evaluated separately for each active goal. Do not apply one global role profile to every sphere.

For each of the player's confirmed goals, build a **goal responsibility map**:

1. Desired outcome: what observable change does the player want?
2. Work packages: what distinct functions are needed to reach it?
3. Player-owned functions: what is the player best placed and willing to do?
4. Support gaps: what needs a different person, specialist, service, tool, or skill?
5. Constraints: budget, time, access, authority, safety, geography, care duties, energy, and dependencies.
6. Ownership: who is responsible for each deliverable and decision?
7. Handoff: what brief, inputs, deadline, and acceptance criteria make delegation workable?
8. Verification: who checks the deliverable and who checks the real-world outcome?
9. Review: what evidence will show that this division of work is helping?

The goal plan should not merely say "delegate". It should help the player identify **what exactly to delegate, to whom, with what authority, what expected output, and how to verify it**.

### Example: creator with weak appetite for administration

The player has a strong concept, understands the system, and can explain what needs to exist. But recurring task assignment, follow-up, reminders, and status control drain attention and repeatedly stall delivery.

A useful recommendation is not "become more disciplined" or "try harder to control people". The game may suggest:
- keep concept, product direction, principles, and key creative decisions with the creator;
- find a producer or project coordinator to translate the concept into owners, milestones, and dependencies;
- add administrative support for recurring follow-up, records, scheduling, and status;
- use specialists for concrete deliverables;
- agree on one short weekly review where the creator makes only decisions that genuinely require their authorship;
- define a simple escalation rule for blocked tasks.

The player may choose one person for several functions in a small team, hire a contractor, use a service, automate part of the process, reduce project scope, or learn one missing skill. The recommendation is a menu of viable structures, not an instruction to hire.

### Example: administrator without a clear concept

A person can coordinate, follow up, and keep delivery moving, but the desired result or product definition is vague. The right support may be a creator, domain expert, strategist, or decision owner who clarifies the direction and acceptance criteria. More tracking will not solve an undefined goal.

### Example: specialist who is overloaded

A specialist may be highly capable at execution but also be carrying administration, stakeholder communication, quality checks, and decisions. The system should surface role overload and suggest narrowing ownership, adding coordination support, or renegotiating scope rather than treating exhaustion as a motivation problem.

## 6. Recommendation ladder: support before hiring

Do not jump straight from a detected gap to "hire an employee". Consider options in a proportionate order:

1. Remove or simplify the task if it does not materially contribute to the goal.
2. Clarify the task and its acceptance criteria.
3. Use an existing tool, template, reminder, or automation.
4. Batch or schedule the task to reduce context switching.
5. Learn the smallest missing skill if the player wants and has capacity.
6. Ask for help or exchange support with someone trusted.
7. Delegate a bounded task to a collaborator.
8. Buy a specialist service or short-term contractor support.
9. Recruit a continuing team member when the need is recurring and justified.

Before suggesting a paid person, check affordability and whether the task is sufficiently defined to delegate. Do not recommend outsourcing confidential or sensitive work without considering privacy and consent.

## 7. Team complementarity and collaboration design

The system can suggest complementary functions, but must not label real people or infer their abilities without the player's evidence.

A collaboration suggestion should be framed as:
- "Для этой части проекта может пригодиться человек, которому комфортно вести операционную координацию."
- "Похоже, тебе сейчас нужен не еще один автор идеи, а тот, кто сможет организовать проверку и доведение до результата."
- "Эту часть можно передать профильному специалисту, если у тебя есть бюджет и понятное техническое задание."

Do not say:
- "Тебе нужен администратор, потому что ты не умеешь доводить дела."
- "Ты создатель, поэтому тебе не подходит управление."
- "Роман — администратор по типу личности" or make claims about a named third party from the player's description.
- "Найми человека, который будет вытряхивать результат любой ценой."

A healthy collaboration brief contains:
- purpose and expected result;
- scope and non-scope;
- named owner for each deliverable;
- decision rights and limits;
- deadline and check-in rhythm;
- access and resources;
- acceptance criteria;
- escalation route;
- compensation / exchange / agreement;
- privacy and confidentiality boundaries;
- a fair process for disagreement and revision.

The game should also help the player notice when a team gap is actually a missing decision, unclear brief, unrealistic deadline, insufficient budget, lack of authority, or structural dependency—not a "wrong person".

## 8. Integration with Goal Execution Pattern Map and Reactor

This map is a supporting lens inside the same personalization system, not a separate test, separate goal, or new daily obligation.

### Before Reactor Start
- Combine goal-execution observations with the goal-specific functional fit map.
- Identify which steps require the player's own action and which require external support.
- Make sure the player can see the difference between "I need to change my strategy" and "I need a resource or another role".
- Present a concise responsibility map and invite edits before explicit Reactor Start.
- Do not start or reset the 28-day calendar merely because the player reviews or edits the map.

### In the goal plan
Every relevant step may carry:
- owner: player / collaborator / specialist / service / tool / shared;
- function required;
- brief or dependency;
- expected deliverable;
- acceptance criteria;
- due date or review point;
- blocker / support request;
- outcome verification.

Not every field needs to be shown in the daily interface. Keep the full plan available in the goal detail.

### In the Reactor
Show contextual prompts only when useful:
- "Это действительно твоя задача или здесь нужен другой участник?"
- "Какую часть результата можешь создать ты, а какую лучше поручить специалисту?"
- "Достаточно ли ясно сформулирована задача, чтобы ее можно было передать?"
- "Кто отвечает за следующий шаг и как вы поймете, что он выполнен?"
- "Ты сейчас пытаешься удержать на себе слишком много разных функций?"
- "Нужен ли тебе человек, который поможет с организацией, а не еще одна практика самоконтроля?"

Track separately:
- player's own action;
- delegated request / agreement;
- collaborator's deliverable (only if the player chooses to record it);
- external outcome;
- pending result / no evidence yet.

Do not count a collaborator's work as the player's own action, but do recognize the player's effective work in defining, selecting, coordinating, resourcing, or verifying the collaboration.

### Workshop connection
The existing Reactor Strategy Workshop can host a small "Распределение функций" exercise inside a goal review. Do not create another top-level tab unless usability testing proves it is needed. It should be an optional exercise within the same strategy workshop.

## 9. Evidence, privacy, and safeguards

- No automated scan of free-text diary.
- No remote analytics or hidden profiling.
- Use structured answers, explicit player-selected examples, and confirmed task history only.
- Keep source, date, version, context, confidence, and player correction.
- Do not use psychotype, DOB matrix, Arcana, BG16, or Echo as proof of functional capacity.
- Do not assume that a person who has not had an opportunity to lead lacks leadership ability.
- Do not confuse lack of authority with lack of ability.
- Do not psychologize poverty, inaccessible services, discrimination, unsafe work, disability, illness, care responsibilities, or other real constraints.
- Do not encourage coercive management, manipulation, exploitation, or unpaid labor.
- Respect third-party privacy: the player can describe what they need from a collaborator, but the game must not create a psychological profile of that person.
- Keep player ownership: accept, edit, decline, or postpone recommendations.
- Do not make hiring, spending, or relationship decisions on the player's behalf.

## 12. Acceptance criteria

The feature is ready for implementation only when:
1. It remains a functional map, not a fixed personality typology.
2. It distinguishes ability, experience, preference, current capacity, and role conditions.
3. It evaluates fit separately for each active goal.
4. It identifies both strengths to preserve and functions that need support.
5. It can recommend learning, simplification, tools, collaboration, services, or delegation—not only hiring.
6. It creates actionable handoffs with owners, deliverables, criteria, and review points.
7. It does not label or profile third parties.
8. It does not blame the player for structural constraints.
9. It feeds the goal plan and existing Reactor Strategy Workshop without adding another mandatory test or daily task.
10. It preserves stable IDs, history, privacy, export/import, and the 28-day calendar.
11. Every recommendation can be edited or declined.
12. Production implementation is separately tested; this document alone does not mean the feature is live.


## 13. Activation and realization conditions: reuse the earlier authorial "projector" model

This layer must explicitly reuse the existing authorial realization model already developed for the game. Do not replace it with a newly invented typology and do not silently reinterpret it as Human Design.

The earlier model was described as an **authorial behavioral / realization framework calculated from date of birth alone**, without birth time or place and without claiming to calculate a Human Design bodygraph. Its named realization patterns were:
- **Projector:** recognition, a concrete invitation or request, and focused participation. A capability may become fully engaged when another person recognizes it and asks for a specific contribution.
- **Generator:** response, sustained engagement, and development through continued involvement.
- **Manifestor:** independent initiation and informing relevant people about the launch.
- **Manifesting Generator (MG):** response, quick trials, changing trajectory when needed, and acceleration through iterative action.
- **Reflector:** environment, observation, and evaluation of conditions before committing.

The earlier framework also discussed an authorial "splenic" layer as quiet, immediate bodily recognition of safety / danger, to be distinguished from anxiety and impulsive urgency. Reuse only the existing approved description and calculation; do not invent a new health, safety, or intuition diagnosis.

**Implementation rule:** inspect the existing birth-profile calculation and stored `P.nature` fields in current source before connecting them. Preserve its current formula, labels, versioning, and outputs. Do not recalculate or change birth-matrix / Arcana logic as part of this feature. If the stored profile cannot support a particular claim, omit that claim rather than infer it.

### 13.1 The key distinction: capacity, role, and activation are different layers

The functional map in Sections 2–7 asks: "Which work functions fit this goal, and what support is needed?"

The realization model asks: "Under what conditions does this person tend to engage their capacity and bring it into the world?"

The goal-execution map asks: "What action, feedback, and verification loop will move this particular goal forward?"

These layers must be combined, not substituted for one another. A Projector-style recognition / invitation cue does not prove that the player lacks initiative, and a Manifestor-style independent-start cue does not mean the player must always work alone. Each is a possible preferred activation route to be checked against lived examples and the player's own correction.

For example, the player may be highly capable of creating concepts and systems, but self-generated projects repeatedly remain private or unrealized. A concrete external request — "Would you write a book?", "Could you design this?", "Would you take on this project?" — can provide a real-world point of entry, recognition of a specific capability, an audience, a scope, and a reason to commit. The game should not translate this into "you cannot start by yourself". It should help the player test whether **a clear invitation or commissioned brief is a more effective activation condition for this goal**.

Likewise, a player who tends to initiate independently may benefit from a clear permission-to-start and an informing step; a response-led player may benefit from real options to respond to rather than abstract pressure to invent a direction; an iterative player may benefit from small trials and room to adjust; an environment-sensitive player may benefit from checking the conditions and context before committing.

### 13.2 Translate activation conditions into practical goal options

For each active goal, the plan may offer a relevant activation route, only when supported by the existing authorial profile and/or player-confirmed examples:

| Activation route | Practical support in Goal Path / Reactor |
|---|---|
| Recognition / invitation / request | Identify people or communities who can see the player's relevant ability; prepare a clear offer or portfolio; invite a concrete brief; seek a commission, referral, open call, collaboration, or direct request; define boundaries so recognition does not become dependence on approval |
| Response / sustained engagement | Create real options to respond to; use a bounded trial or conversation; identify what produces genuine engagement; build a sustainable rhythm and visible progress |
| Independent initiation | Define the first self-authorized move; decide who needs to be informed; create a small launch rather than waiting for universal approval |
| Quick trials / iterative adjustment | Set up a short experiment, fast feedback, and explicit permission to change direction without treating every pivot as failure |
| Environment / conditions assessment | Check people, setting, resources, timing, and constraints; use observation and a reversible test before making a larger commitment |

These are prompts, not prescriptions. The player can choose another route or say the description does not fit.

### 13.3 Activation is not the same as delegation

An invitation can activate the player's own work without transferring authorship or responsibility to the inviter. A commission can provide a brief and a real audience; the player may still own the concept and execution. Conversely, a person may have a strong activation route but still need an administrator, producer, specialist, or executor to complete the work.

Keep three questions separate:
1. **What activates my contribution?** Invitation, response, self-initiation, experimentation, environment, or another player-confirmed condition.
2. **What function do I contribute best?** Creation, architecture, coordination, administration, execution, research, communication, verification, etc.
3. **What support is needed around my contribution?** A collaborator, producer, administrator, specialist, tool, service, budget, skill, or changed scope.

### 13.4 Tracker prompts: make the insight actionable

Do not merely show the player's realization profile as a static portrait. Carry it into the relevant goal and Reactor moments. Possible prompts:
- "Ты сейчас ждешь, что кто-то сам заметит твою способность. Кому можно показать конкретный результат или предложить понятный формат сотрудничества?"
- "Тебе легче раскрыться, когда появляется внешний запрос. Какой реальный запрос, заказ или приглашение могло бы открыть путь к этому желанию?"
- "Не обязательно снова пытаться запустить весь проект в одиночку. Кто может дать ему первый внешний контекст — заказчик, партнер, редактор, сообщество?"
- "Что именно человек должен у тебя попросить, чтобы твоя сильная сторона включилась?"
- "Есть ли сейчас реальный отклик, или ты пытаешься заставить себя продолжать без интереса и обратной связи?"
- "Какой небольшой самостоятельный запуск ты можешь сделать, если ждать приглашения сейчас не от кого?"
- "Можно ли сначала проверить эту идею короткой пробой, а затем решить, стоит ли менять направление?"
- "Подходит ли тебе среда и условия, в которых ты собираешься это делать?"
- "Тебе нужен новый способ действовать — или сначала внешний запрос, партнер, доступ, аудитория либо конкретная задача?"

Prompts must not shame the player, create passivity, or imply that a particular invitation will necessarily arrive. Where an external request is not available, offer an agency-preserving alternative: make capability visible, contact a relevant person, publish a sample, send a proposal, join a community, or design a small self-initiated experiment.

### 13.5 Trigger points in the Reactor

Use the activation layer selectively:
- **Before the first goal step:** help the player choose a realistic activation route for this goal.
- **When a goal remains in preparation:** distinguish lack of clarity/skill/resources from a missing external brief, audience, invitation, or response opportunity.
- **When the player repeatedly starts private projects that do not reach an audience:** consider a visibility, commission, collaboration, or request experiment, but only as an option.
- **When a real request arrives:** help define scope, boundaries, deliverable, capacity, price/terms where relevant, and a next action.
- **At weekly review:** compare which conditions actually increased engagement and progress.
- **When the route does not work:** revise the hypothesis; do not insist that the player must wait for an invitation or force a self-start.

Do not turn every day into a personality reminder. The prompt should appear only at a useful decision point and remain dismissible.

### 13.6 Evidence and boundaries

- The date-based authorial realization profile is a starting lens for prompts, not proof of behavior, ability, destiny, or a guaranteed outcome.
- Keep it distinct from the 19-state model, BG16, Echo adaptation strategies, and the goal-execution evidence map.
- Do not derive role competence from the realization label.
- Do not use the profile to decide which desires are legitimate or which goals the player may pursue.
- Player-confirmed examples and current goal context take priority over a generic profile description.
- Do not change the earlier calculation or require new birth data.
- Preserve the ability to say "this description does not fit me" and use a neutral plan.
